The Joy of Missing Out: How to Embrace JOMO for True Relaxation

2026-08-04 · Relaxation · Tags: The, selfcaresunday, relaxation

In a world that never stops buzzing, the pressure to stay connected, informed, and present in every digital conversation can feel overwhelming. We scroll through feeds filled with highlights of others' lives, attend virtual events we don't really want to join, and respond to messages out of obligation rather than desire. This constant state of alertness—fueled by the fear of missing out (FOMO)—keeps our nervous systems on edge, making true relaxation nearly impossible. But there is a counter-movement, a quiet rebellion that invites us to step off the treadmill of perpetual engagement. It's called JOMO—the Joy of Missing Out. And it might just be the most radical self-care practice you can adopt this year.

The FOMO Epidemic and Its Toll on Your Nervous System

FOMO isn't just a social anxiety; it's a physiological response. When we see others having experiences we're not part of, our brains interpret it as a threat to our social standing. This triggers the release of cortisol, the stress hormone, and activates the sympathetic nervous system—our fight-or-flight response. Over time, chronic FOMO keeps us in a state of low-grade hypervigilance, where we're constantly checking our phones, refreshing feeds, and scanning for the next opportunity to feel included. This state is the antithesis of relaxation. It fragments our attention, disrupts our sleep, and leaves us feeling depleted even when we're not doing anything physically demanding.

The irony is that the more we try to keep up, the more we miss out on the present moment. We're so busy documenting our lives for others that we forget to live them. We attend concerts through our phone screens, have conversations while half-listening to notifications, and eat meals while scrolling through other people's food photos. This constant multitasking not only reduces our enjoyment but also impairs our ability to truly rest. Our brains never get the signal that it's safe to power down, because there's always another update, another story, another event to catch.

What JOMO Really Means

JOMO is not about being antisocial or rejecting all forms of connection. It's about making a conscious choice to prioritize your own well-being over the fear of being left out. It's the recognition that you cannot do everything, see everything, or be everywhere—and that's perfectly okay. JOMO is the liberating realization that missing out on certain things actually creates space for more meaningful experiences. It's about shifting your focus from what you're not doing to what you are doing, and finding joy in the simple, unplugged moments that often go unnoticed.

At its core, JOMO is a practice of intentionality. It asks you to define what truly matters to you—your values, your relationships, your passions—and then align your time and energy accordingly. When you embrace JOMO, you stop measuring your worth by how many events you attend or how many likes you get. Instead, you measure it by the depth of your presence, the quality of your connections, and the peace you feel in your own skin. This shift in perspective is not only relaxing but also empowering, because it puts you back in the driver's seat of your own life.

Practical Steps to Cultivate JOMO

Embracing JOMO doesn't happen overnight. It requires a deliberate unlearning of the habits that keep you tethered to the digital world. Here are some actionable steps to start your journey:

  • Conduct a digital declutter: Go through your apps, subscriptions, and social media follows. Unfollow accounts that trigger feelings of inadequacy or FOMO. Turn off non-essential notifications—especially those from social media, news, and group chats. Keep only the ones that genuinely add value to your life.
  • Schedule intentional unplugging: Set aside specific times each day when you are completely offline. This could be during meals, the first hour after waking, or the last hour before bed. Use this time to engage in activities that ground you—reading, journaling, stretching, or simply sitting in silence.
  • Practice the art of the polite decline: When you receive an invitation that doesn't excite you, you have permission to say no. You don't need to offer a lengthy explanation. A simple, "Thank you for thinking of me, but I need some time to recharge," is enough. Remember, every yes to something is a no to something else—often your own peace.
  • Create a "missing out" list: Write down the things you're deliberately choosing to miss out on—whether it's a TV series everyone's talking about, a networking event, or a social media trend. Acknowledge that you're making a choice, and then let it go. This helps you see that missing out is a conscious decision, not a failure.

These steps are not about deprivation; they're about liberation. By reducing the noise, you give yourself the gift of clarity and calm.

Designing Your JOMO Ritual

To truly integrate JOMO into your life, it helps to create a ritual—a dedicated time and space where you celebrate missing out. This could be a weekly "JOMO hour" or a monthly "unplugged afternoon." The key is to make it a sacred appointment with yourself, something you look forward to rather than dread.

Start by choosing a time that works for you. Maybe it's Sunday evening, when the week ahead looms large, or Saturday morning, when you can linger over coffee without rushing. During this ritual, you commit to being fully present in your own world. Turn off your phone, close your laptop, and let your mind wander. You might take a walk in nature, cook a meal from scratch, or simply sit on your porch and watch the clouds. The activity doesn't matter as much as the intention: to be exactly where you are, without the urge to document or share.

To make your JOMO ritual even more powerful, consider adding a sensory element. Light a candle with a calming scent, play soft instrumental music, or wrap yourself in a cozy blanket. These cues signal to your body that it's time to relax. You can also incorporate a gratitude practice—write down three things you're grateful for that you might have missed if you'd been busy elsewhere. This shifts your focus from lack to abundance, reinforcing the joy of missing out.

The Unexpected Gifts of Missing Out

When you embrace JOMO, you'll start to notice a cascade of positive effects that go beyond just feeling less stressed. One of the first gifts is a deeper sense of presence. Without the constant pull of your phone, you'll find yourself fully engaged in conversations, savoring your meals, and noticing the small details of your environment that you previously overlooked. This heightened awareness is a form of mindfulness that naturally reduces anxiety and increases contentment.

Another gift is the resurgence of creativity. When your mind is not constantly bombarded with external stimuli, it has the space to make new connections, generate ideas, and solve problems. Many people report that their best ideas come during moments of boredom or daydreaming—exactly the kind of mental state that JOMO encourages. By allowing yourself to miss out on the noise, you open the door to your own inner wisdom.

Finally, JOMO improves your relationships. When you're not half-present, you can give your full attention to the people who matter most. You'll find that you have more energy for meaningful conversations, more patience for loved ones, and more capacity for empathy. And as you model this behavior, you give others permission to unplug too, creating a ripple effect of calm in your social circle.

Conclusion

The joy of missing out is not about hiding from the world; it's about showing up for yourself in a way that honors your need for rest and renewal. In a culture that glorifies busyness and constant connectivity, choosing to miss out is a revolutionary act of self-care. It's a declaration that your peace is more important than your presence in every digital conversation, that your well-being is not negotiable, and that you are worthy of rest without guilt. So the next time you feel the pull of FOMO, pause and ask yourself: What am I really missing? More often than not, the answer is nothing—and that's the most liberating truth of all. Start small, be gentle with yourself, and watch as the joy of missing out transforms your relationship with relaxation, one unplugged moment at a time.
